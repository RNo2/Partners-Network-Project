## Methodology and limitations · version 1

**Purpose.** Compare going deep, going wide, and network coordination. Location patterns, community need, partner review, and feasibility are distinct evidence. The system does not generate closure recommendations, effectiveness rankings, or estimated marginal impacts.

**Provenance and persistence.** SQLite schema migration 1 defines standardized tables. Stable identifiers and foreign keys link sites and activity. Source labels, indicator definitions, periods and original upload bytes are retained. Every accepted import stores a timestamp, counts, mode, semantic fingerprint and validation report. Prior raw versions remain available; current records carry their latest import ID. Failed imports are rolled back; their validation report can be downloaded before import. Failed attempts are not stored in import history. No rejected row is silently accepted or dropped.

**Imports.** Map fields explicitly, review row errors and warnings, then confirm append, update or replace. Update accepts existing IDs only. Replace synchronizes one category; it cannot remove referenced sites. Semantic repeat imports are blocked even under a different filename. Coordinate, date, numeric, enum, required-field, row-ID and reference checks occur before commit. Overlapping distribution intervals for the same site and duplicate indicator observations are blocked transactionally. Nonidentical IDs do not prove two differently named records are different physical providers: directory reconciliation still requires review.

**Community need.** Rates are recomputed from supplied numerator and denominator where present. Weighted aggregation pools compatible numerators and denominators. Missing denominators withhold aggregate rates; source rates remain visible. Counts sum only within the selected resolution, definition, source, vintage and year; selected units must not overlap. The application does not invent crosswalks or automatically downscale estimates. Census uncertainty is not modeled yet. Indicators at different resolutions, years or sources are never pooled automatically.

**Geography.** GeoJSON and GeoPackage uploads use their declared CRS and are transformed to WGS84. Only valid polygons are accepted; boundary versions are selected explicitly. Map display uses Web Mercator with offline Plotly geometry, without external tiles or geographic downloads. Maps are schematic analytical views, not navigational maps. Provider affiliation is supplied as Share, external or unknown, never inferred. A provider located on a shared polygon boundary can count in both polygons; polygon counts should not be summed as unique regional totals. Boundary holes and overlap should be reviewed in source GIS.

**Coverage.** Active, coordinate-bearing records are matched with polygon `covers`. Great-circle distances use Earth radius 6371.0088 km and a user-selected exploratory radius. Near-area counts use a polygon interior representative point, not a household/population centroid. They are preliminary geographic proxies, not walking distances, transit times or demonstrated unmet demand. Unlocated and unknown-status sites are excluded from coverage and disclosed in the overview. Zero documented locations does not prove absence of services. Uploaded route times retain travel mode, type, source and date. The application does not perform transit routing or infer transit access from stops.

**Schedules.** Effective dates and verification status filter source windows. Weekly calculations use only frequency `weekly`; irregular and monthly schedules remain in the source table and are not converted to fabricated dates. Same-site overlapping windows are merged before summing hours. Selected-area schedule and pair comparisons use a spatial index over a polygon buffer in NAD83 / UTM zone 18N (EPSG:26918), appropriate to the three-county study region. The buffer is a geographic screening tool, not a walking service area. Pairwise overlap intersects and unions windows for nearby sites on the same day. Missing schedules mean unknown availability, not zero. Blank effective dates are unbounded, not proof of current verification. A selected effective date identifies schedule validity, not a promise that every listed weekday operates on that date. Day maps may show source-reported nonweekly windows; check the frequency column. Scheduled availability is not utilization.

**Distribution.** Summaries group exact start/end intervals and include reporting-site counts per metric. Incompatible intervals remain separate. Missing quantities are not filled with zero. Period totals with incomplete site reporting are partial totals. Household/individual counts may include repeat visits and cannot be called unique people. Received versus distributed may reflect inventories or reporting differences, not waste. Additional pounds sent is distinct from pounds received.

**Partner assessment.** Criteria, framework versions, guidance and approval status are source-provided. Notes, additional services, reach, dates and review statuses can be imported now. Ratings require a matching approved criterion/version. No scores, weights, default standards or partner rankings exist. Approval status is administrative metadata and requires organizational governance; this local application has no authenticated approval workflow.

**Resource scenarios.** Staff-entered rows describe target, action, incremental pounds, evidence, intended benefit, capacity and unknowns. Deep and optimization target existing Share partners; wide targets an uploaded area. The application checks the same-period food budget and records the input import IDs. It does not calculate benefits, determine feasibility, optimize routes, choose sites, or recommend consolidation. Warehouse, food variety, transport, staff and relationship capacity must be reviewed outside the budget check. Scenario comparisons across reporting intervals are not like-for-like.

**Prioritization engine.** Independently tested Python API only, pending an approved UI/governance workflow. Explicit approval, version, resolution, weights summing to one, direction, minimum weighted coverage and missing-data policy are required. Min-max uses the supplied comparison cohort; a constant indicator has neutral value 0.5. Withhold blocks incomplete scores; renormalize rescales observed contributions and discloses coverage. No imputation to zero. Alternative configurations support sensitivity comparisons. No approved configuration is installed and no rankings are displayed. Analysis run/configuration tables reserve provenance for future integration; scores are not currently persisted by the UI.

**Privacy.** Defaults are local-loopback hosting with telemetry disabled. Internal data stays in SQLite; public-data view reads public-labeled records and disables management, distribution, assessment and scenario access. This is a defense in depth preview, not an authenticated multiuser public deployment. For public publication, create a separate reviewed public-only database/artifact and audit it for confidential data; never distribute an internal database or its raw imports. The app has not been deployed.

## V1.0 public release changes

The separate `public_app.py` entrypoint reads versioned Parquet through a mutation-free public backend. Public release assets and source coverage are documented in Public Data Status; the earlier empty-foundation statement applies only to the original local milestone. The public UI includes sourced community estimates and provider evidence, not internal data.

Public coverage includes documented records with unknown operating status, excludes source-declared inactive sites, and clearly retains source status. Exact-address shared-location candidates are flagged, not merged. Benefits-assistance offices are not food distribution sites and are excluded from the food-provider view by default. Non-Share directory labels are not proof of independence.

Public weekly visualizations use **source-published, unverified** structured windows. The earlier verified-only rule continues to describe the internal workflow. Exception-bearing windows are retained as source text, not converted to assumed weekly opportunities. MAHN and county PDF hours remain readable source text when not safely parsed. No verification date is synthesized from retrieval dates.

ACS source estimates and 90% MOEs are exposed; derived rates do not have calculated MOEs. Median household income cannot be summed across tracts. USDA retailer access is in a separate Community Need panel with original variable dictionaries. Source demographic years and tract vintages must not be substituted for ACS 2024 values.

SEPTA maps show actual stop points and representative route shapes. Calendars, trips and stop times are retained in read-only assets for future analysis, not interpreted as real-time journeys or proof of pantry accessibility. Public strategic scenarios compare actual area indicators without saving visitor records or calculating invented food/financial outcomes.
